Getting started

What is stream?

A free app for classic films and live TV channels. It runs in your browser and can be installed on your phone, tablet or laptop.

Is it really free?

Yes. We may show ads in the future to cover running costs.

How do I install it?

On Android, open the browser menu and tap Install app or Add to Home screen. On iPhone, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. On a laptop, use the install icon in Chrome’s address bar.

Your account

How do I sign up?

Tap Get started, then enter your name, date of birth, email and a password, or choose Continue with Google.

I forgot my password.

Go to the sign-in page, type your email, then tap Forgot password. We’ll email you a reset link.

Can I change my name or delete my account?

Yes. Open Profile, then Account. You can change your account name or delete your account there. To rename a profile or change its avatar, use Settings.

Is my information safe?

Your password is handled by Google Firebase and we can’t see it. Read our Privacy Policy to see what we collect and why.

Watching films

A film won’t play or keeps buffering.

Films stream from the Internet Archive, which can be slow at busy times. Check your connection, refresh the page, or try again a little later. Wi-Fi usually works better than weak mobile data.

Where do the films come from?

From the Internet Archive’s public-domain collection. See our Legal and content notice.

Does it remember where I stopped?

Yes, it saves your place in each film on the device you are using. Syncing across devices is coming later.

Why doesn’t my iPhone turn sideways when I press play?

On Android, fullscreen turns the screen sideways automatically. iPhones don’t allow websites to do that, so rotate your phone yourself.

Live TV

A channel doesn’t work.

Channels come from third parties. Some only work in their own country and some go offline or change their address. Try another channel, or come back later.

How do I turn on captions?

Tap the CC button in the player. It only appears when the channel provides captions, and most films don’t have them.

Why is there no schedule for some channels?

Schedules come from public programme guides, which don’t cover every channel.
